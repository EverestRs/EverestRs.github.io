While many existing methods generate HDR images from 8-bit low dynamic range (LDR) sRGB inputs, these images have already lost critical data during the camera’s processing pipeline. Attempting to reconstruct extreme dynamic ranges from this compressed format is inherently limited. Working with raw sensor data offers a better alternative because it preserves more original scene information. However, current deep learning approaches for Raw-to-HDR mapping struggle with two main issues. First, they often apply static fusion across all channels, ignoring the fact that the Bayer pattern samples green pixels twice as often as red or blue. Second, they struggle to hallucinate textures in severely saturated regions, which often causes color artifacts and lost details. To solve this, we design a single-image HDR framework built around the specific properties of raw data. We introduce an Adaptive Green Prior Modulation Module (AGPM) that explicitly uses the denser green channel to guide the initial spatial reconstruction. We then use a Dual Branch Guidance Enhancement (DBGE) module and a Green-Channel Guidance Transformer (GCGT) to pass this structural knowledge to the red and blue channels. For blown-out areas, our Content-Aware Reconstruction Network (CARNet) relies on estimated exposure masks to restore plausible details. Tests confirm our approach outperforms existing single-image HDR methods on standard datasets.}
